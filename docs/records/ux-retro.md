## Measurement protocol

The primary criterion is whether a grandmother can understand and use the product from the screen alone. For each change, record the task, persona, clicks and keystrokes, first-load and navigation time, completion and hesitations before and after under the same conditions, including whether the grandmother task was completed. Treat simulated impressions as hypotheses, not user testimony. Use three grounded scenarios: a person using a phone who needs clear language; a first-time user who uses only a 360 px phone, knows no development terms and receives only the task with no explanation (the grandmother); and a person using only a 360 px phone. A grandmother task passes only when an actual person matching that definition completes it without explanation; an AI walk can expose failure signals but cannot establish a pass. Compare zoom, undo, Escape and dragging with familiar tools after checking their actual behavior. Review all ten Nielsen heuristics. A change is not validated until the same tasks are repeated; investigate or revert a change that fails to improve its measure.

AI walkthroughs are failure signals, never proof that a person can complete a task; record human validation separately. Before and after each relevant change, check: (1) repeat-load content within 2.5 seconds, response within 0.2 seconds and progress for waits over 1 second; (2) fit, buttons, pinch and Control-wheel; (3) keyboard and button undo; (4) alternatives to dragging; (5) confirmation target and consequence; (6) current and next step through acceptance, reflection, confirmation and recording; (7) consistent explained action words; (8) empty-stage next action; (9) phone task or a local explanation and alternative; (10) finding a named screen and first click; (11) 16 px body text, 4.5:1 contrast and 44 px main targets; (12) Escape from any menu focus; and (13) named recipients for replies and new comments. Measure rather than assume; targets are proposed evaluation criteria, not changes to approved requirements.
